## Problem
Quantum computing is advancing rapidly toward a threshold — commonly referred to as "Q-Day" — at which current cryptographic algorithms that protect virtually all digital communications and stored data will become breakable. The timeline to reach quantum readiness far exceeds the time organizations have left.

* NIST finalized the first post-quantum cryptography standards in 2024, and regulatory bodies including the U.S. government and the Bank of Israel have issued guidance requiring organizations to begin migrating to PQC. The U.S. mandate calls for sensitive information connections to be encrypted with PQC by 2027, with most assets finalized by 2030
* However, the typical enterprise PQC migration takes five to ten years to complete — creating a dangerous gap between the timeline of the quantum threat and the pace of organizational readiness
* Most organizations fundamentally lack visibility into their own cryptographic posture: they do not know what cryptographic algorithms they use, where those algorithms are applied, what data they protect, or how cryptographic assets interconnect across their infrastructure
* State-sponsored and sophisticated adversaries are already conducting "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks — capturing encrypted data today with the intention of decrypting it once quantum computing capabilities mature
* Existing security tools were not designed to inventory and manage cryptography as a distinct asset class. The result is a fragmented, hidden, and unmanaged cryptographic landscape where outdated protocols, weak cipher suites, unencrypted databases, and policy violations accumulate without detection
* The transition to PQC is not a one-time migration but an ongoing lifecycle management challenge that requires continuous discovery, policy enforcement, and governance across applications, protocols, third-party components, and data stores

## Product
QIZ Security provides the Cryptography Management Platform, an end-to-end system for discovering, prioritizing, remediating, and governing cryptographic risk across the enterprise:

* Discover — Maps cryptographic assets against organizational policy, revealing vulnerabilities and dependencies across the full stack. The platform provides visibility into cryptography embedded in services, protocols, applications, code, third-party components, and data stores — covering both data in transit between systems and data at rest inside databases and storage. Creates a contextual knowledge graph that connects scattered cryptographic complexity into a unified, navigable map
* Prioritize — Ranks every cryptographic finding by context, impact, severity, and remediation effort. Rather than overwhelming teams with scattered alerts, QIZ delivers a prioritized, step-by-step action plan that cuts through what the company calls "crypto fog"
* Remediate — Provides guided remediation plans to mitigate cryptographic risk, including recommendations for which post-quantum cryptographic algorithms to map to for PQC migration. Surfaces specific issues such as weak cipher suites, outdated TLS configurations, unencrypted databases, and policy violations
* Govern — Builds ongoing cryptographic resilience and agility across the organization through continuous discovery and policy enforcement, replacing manual crypto governance with automation that reduces human error and accelerates compliance

The platform is designed as an API-first, agentless solution — no probes, no agents, just APIs that plug into existing enterprise infrastructure. It supports multi-stakeholder collaboration across CISOs, compliance teams, and application owners, and provides end-to-end lifecycle management from on-premises to cloud with no silos or blind spots. QIZ is backed by Bessemer Venture Partners, Merlin Ventures, and the AWS & CrowdStrike Cybersecurity Accelerator.

## Outcome
I was genuinely excited about QIZ Security for several reasons:

* Stellar founder: Ben Volkow is a rare caliber of entrepreneur — backed by Bessemer Venture Partners three times across three separate companies (Traffix, Otonomo, and now QIZ), with a track record that includes a $135M acquisition by F5 Networks and taking Otonomo public on NASDAQ at a $1.4B valuation. His ability to repeatedly identify large market opportunities, attract tier-1 capital, and scale enterprise businesses to meaningful exits gave exceptional conviction in the founding team
* Macro tailwinds: Quantum computing continues to make tangible advancements and represents a real and growing threat to current cryptographic algorithms. With NIST finalizing PQC standards, U.S. government mandates setting 2027 and 2030 deadlines, and "harvest now, decrypt later" attacks already underway, the window for enterprises to act is compressing. This is a structural market catalyst, not a speculative one
* Impressive product: The platform's ability to map and understand the cryptography of data at rest and in transit across an enterprise — and then recommend which new PQC algorithms to migrate to — was extremely compelling. The discovery and contextual knowledge graph approach addresses a problem that most organizations cannot solve with existing tools
* Enterprise pipeline: QIZ was in active discussion with a large number of enterprise partners for pilots across multiple industries, including automotive and financial services, indicating broad applicability and early demand signal

That said, the partners ultimately did not execute the investment due to concerns about competitive dynamics with an existing portfolio company that was primarily focused on PQC migration for digital assets. While I personally disagreed with this assessment — given the differentiated breadth of QIZ's cryptography management approach versus a narrower digital-asset focus — the overlap concern prevented the partnership from moving forward.
